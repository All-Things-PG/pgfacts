# About The Developer

### Developer Experience Summary

Dave DePottie is a software engineering professional with more than 40 years of experience. He serves as Chairman and IT Director of All Things PG and has worked with websites and databases since the early days of commercial development tools.

After earning a BSc from Brock University in Canada in 1984, Dave held roles including Senior Developer, North American Project Leader, Manager of Professional Services, Systems Analyst, Systems Administrator, DBA/Database Specialist, and Senior Software Engineer. He has worked with large corporations and industry leaders throughout his career.

In his most recent role, Dave managed a six-person team in India during a multi-year migration of scientific and medical publication databases to AWS. The migration involved more than 60 million records from sources including PubMed (Medline), Web of Science, ScienceWire, Journal Citation Reports, and worldwide USPTO patent and grant databases.

You may review Dave's bio at [Dave DePottie](https://www.allthingspg.org/dave-depottie), and please check out the bios of all out [Board Members](https://www.allthingspg.org/our-people). <br>

### Notes from the Developer (Dave DePottie)

<img src="/assets/images/dave-depottie.png" alt="Dave DePottie" style="float: left; width: 220px; max-width: 40%; margin: 0 1.25rem 1rem 0; border-radius: 8px;" />

I envision a website where users come to us for help, and to solve problems. It's not just about information anymore. There's lots of information out there than can be googled or searched by AI.The focus now must be on solutions.  

**PG is much more than a skin disease**, and affects all areas of your life.  My entire life has been altered on this Physical, Emotional and Spiritual Journey. As an experienced patient with PG, I can tell you that PG takes over your life, and leads you down many paths and not all of them are good.  There are alwyas so many issues to address and try to solve, especially if your in pain. Its easy to get lost and not know what to do, or where to turn for help or SOLUTIONS.  A  large FB Support Groups is an execllet place to go and get a virtual HUG.  They become your second family when you need them. All Things PG is not a support group and we cannot recommend any particular group.  Go to facebook and find one now if you haven't already done so.  ATPG is about interactions with the outside work and our PG community.  People, Organiztions, Information, Medical Profesionls, Pharrmaceuticals and mor, AND what they do.  We build relationships.

We want to show a list of associations, clinical trials, research opportunites, PFACs, a powerful Clinic Finder, patient success stories, topics on wound care, supplies, dealing with insurance companies, apply for disability... the potential for information and solutions is endless. Are we ambitious?  You bet.  PG doesn't wait. Thi is why [www.allthingspg.org](https://www.allthingspg.org) is so important.

Our website needs to be more than just information, because users may NOT know the right questions to ask.  Many visitors will be newly diagnosed patients search for answers. I believe that with a good design, our website can offer a guided experience that will lead them to the help they need, particularly through the use of a fully searchable Knowledge Base (KB).

A user could be a new patient or new caregiver, a doctor treating PG for the first time, a pharmaceutical representative curious how to do clinical trials for PG patients.  The number of scenarios is endless, and yet there is only one website.  In order to be all things about PG, we need to support whoever might be visiting the site and why.  Our current website gaves us a a great PG Foundation presense on the web, but the content is static and limited, and its up to the user to find the solution.

With Phase 2 and an enhanced user experience, we can guide someone to the information they need and how to apply it. First we need to know who is visiting the site, so the site must allow selecting their user experience to tell us who they, are without having to register. The central goal objective or goal of Phase 2 is to create a searchable Knowledge Base of accurate, vetted, curated content on any topic, for every type of user.  The more content we collect the better.  Search results will show Menu Items connected to Content that is contained in the Knowledge Base.

From my own personal experience, I started PG way back in 2015 and became disabled diwahled in 2018.  My wife became my caregiver overnight and was lost as to what to do. She saw me in pain and bedridden, but couldn't help. She didn't know how to treat my wounds, but learned and became an expert and a teacher. She researched the internet, found what she could, not all of it was consistent or accurate. Much of the information was out of date. She kept digging non-stop and joined a large  fB group for PG. She shared everything she learned with the group, and she took care of me, which was a 24/7 job. My family had to chip in to cover 8 hr shifts, so that I was never left alone, even in the hospital. It wa a journey for all of us, but somehow we got through it. Not everyone has the same thirst and ability for knowledge as my wife and others, but we care for each other, support each other, and are equally motivated.  A Caregiver should be able to come to the website, read caregiver stuff. Stories, videos on wound care, and consult the knowledge base, as a caregiver.  I shared that thirst for knowledge and learned everything I could about PG, and we  both became very active on the FB support trying to help others learn as well. My vision for the Phase 2 website is to make it a resource that helps everyone.

For me, Phase 2 is about making it easier for the next caregiver, or a new patient, or a provider that has never treated PG before. How can we help a provider? We are not doctors but experienced patients. Doctors can read our stories, see our pictures, and search our knowledge base to understand the emotional journey. Our current website already has valuable, trusted information about diagnosis and treatment, but we can do so much more. We need an easy way to add content, examples, stories, pictures, videos and more. All of this content, linked to menu items, will go into the knowledge base. A user of the site will enter a search and quickly get a list of all articles in the system.  That will be the core of Phase 2, coupled with an enhanced user experience, and a database to support all the new features.   A database is a critical step to offering solutions not just information. My hope is that anyone, regardless of who they are, will not make the mistakes I and my doctors made that sent me down a painful path.

Thank you,
