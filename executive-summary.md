# ATPG Executive Summary

## Executive Summary

All Things PG (ATPG) successfully established an initial web presence for Pyoderma Gangrenosum (PG) in September of 2025. www.allthingspg.org provided foundational support primarily for patients and caregivers. The current website fulfills an important early mission: creating visibility for a newly formed nonprofit organization, providing essential disease information, and establishing credibility within a rare disease community for PG. Phase 1 of the website has been well received.

As ATPG matures, however, the organization's goals and opportunities expand beyond what the original website was designed to support. ATPG now participates in research initiatives, clinical trial discussions, drug development programs, professional education, continued advocacy efforts, conference activities, industry engagements and disease-aware programs like fundraising, newsletters and PFACs. While the current website remains a valuable asset, it no longer fully reflects the expanding scope of ATPG's mission and long-term objectives. As ATPG grows, so will the website.

The proposed direction is not simply the development of a new website. The objective is to create a trusted, searchable, curated knowledge base dedicated to pyoderma gangrenosum. This knowledge base would continuously collect, organize, review, and publish information related to diagnosis, treatment, wound care, clinical trials, research studies, publications, providers, conferences, patient resources, caregiver resources, advocacy initiatives, and emerging therapies.

The primary goal is to help people solve problems rather than simply locate information. Every visitor arrives with a specific need. A newly diagnosed patient may be asking, "What is PG?" A caregiver may be asking, "How can I help my spouse cope with this disease?" A provider may be seeking diagnostic guidance or treatment information. Researchers and industry partners may be looking for current studies, clinical trials, unmet needs, or emerging therapeutic opportunities.

A searchable knowledge base allows ATPG to organize information around those needs and guide visitors toward answers rather than requiring them to understand the structure of the website. For example, a caregiver seeking help with wound care could search the knowledge base and receive a curated collection of related resources, including introductory information about PG, wound-care guidance, pain-management resources, frequently asked questions, patient and caregiver experiences, educational content, and provider recommendations. In this model, the goal is not to help users find pages. The goal is to help users find answers.

Beyond its value as an educational resource, ATPG has an opportunity to become a central knowledge-sharing hub within the PG ecosystem. By organizing and curating information related to clinical trials, emerging therapies, research studies, publications, provider resources, and patient experiences, ATPG can help bridge information gaps that currently exist among patients, caregivers, providers, researchers, advocacy organizations, and industry partners. In a rare disease community where knowledge is often fragmented and difficult to discover, a trusted platform for sharing information has the potential to accelerate awareness, improve education, support research participation, and increase visibility into ongoing therapeutic development.

A curated knowledge platform creates opportunities to serve different stakeholder groups, while maintaining ATPG's role as an independent and trusted source of information. Patients and caregivers may access practical guidance and educational resources. Providers may access clinical references, research summaries, and treatment information. Researchers and industry partners may access information related to current studies, clinical trials, disease awareness initiatives, and opportunities for collaboration. By connecting these audiences through a common knowledge platform, ATPG can play a meaningful role in advancing understanding of PG and supporting progress toward improved treatments and outcomes, resulting in new collaborations that may ultimately lead to new drug development.

Importantly, this vision does not require discarding ATPG's current investment in content, branding, or organizational identity. All existing content can be preserved, migrated, refined, and then expanded over time. The objective is not to replace ATPG's mission, but to provide a stronger platform for delivering it in the future. Central to Phase 2 will be the development of a database to store content, patient registries, newsletters, emails sent and received, and much more. The Phase 2 website will be data-driven, which is the opposite of static pages. Content for each page is stored in the knowledge base and delivered to the website as an HTML page. Appearance wise, it will look the similar as the current website, but the curated content can easily be expanded and tailored to fit the specific needs of the user visiting the site. A data-driven process offers solutions, and not just information.

Ultimately, success should not be measured by the technology itself. Success is achieved when ATPG becomes a place that people can trust when seeking reliable information about PG: a resource that helps patients and caregivers navigate a difficult disease, supports provider education and access to research studies, promotes PG awareness throughout the world, highlights clinical trials, facilitates knowledge sharing, and contributes to the long-term advancement of enhanced knowledge within the PG community. 

In summary, ATPG's opportunity is to evolve from a website containing static information about PG, into a live, trusted, searchable knowledge base of curated information that connects patients, caregivers, providers, researchers, and industry partners around a shared mission: Improving the lives of those affected by PG and Raising PG awareness on a global scale. We truly want All Things PG to be ALL the things we can do to help those suffering from this rare disease. Phase 2 is a huge leap forward towards making that happen.
