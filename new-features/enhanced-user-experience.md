# Enhanced User Experience

Phase 2 is not just about adding more content. It is about making the experience more useful for the person who is visiting the site.

The same topic can be presented differently depending on who is viewing it. For example, a caregiver may need practical guidance about wound care, daily support, and what to watch for at home. A doctor may need a more clinical presentation with diagnostic details, treatment considerations, and links to research or related resources.

That difference matters. The site should help each visitor feel like the content was prepared for them, without forcing everyone through the same generic path.

### What that looks like

- A caregiver may see plain-language guidance, support options, and next steps.
- A patient may see symptom information, coping ideas, and ways to find help.
- A provider may see clinical references, treatment context, and supporting resources.
- A researcher or industry visitor may see trials, publications, and structured information.

The goal is to present the right kind of information at the right time, so visitors can move forward more quickly and with less confusion.

### Why this helps

This approach makes the site feel more thoughtful and more relevant. It reduces the need for visitors to sift through pages that do not match what they need right now.

It also helps the organization by making the site easier to curate. Instead of one long page trying to serve everyone equally, Phase 2 can provide a guided experience that changes based on the visitor's purpose.

### What the proof of concept shows

The proof of concept already demonstrates this idea in action. The same content model can support different views of the site, so the visitor experience can be adjusted without rebuilding everything from scratch.
