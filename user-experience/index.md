### One site, different paths

A person newly diagnosed with Pyoderma Gangrenosum, the family member helping with daily care, and a clinician encountering PG for the first time may all need information about the same subject. They do not necessarily need the same explanation, level of detail, or next step.

The portal concept gives each visitor a way into the shared site that reflects their purpose. A visitor can choose Patient, Caregiver, Provider, Pharmaceutical, or Guest. The site can then present a fitting landing page and navigation path while remaining part of one All Things PG experience.

### What the POC shows

The POC demonstrates the portal pattern through a shared site shell, an experience selector, audience-specific landing pages, and navigation menus that vary by visitor experience. The patient and provider examples show how the starting content and menu can differ.

These screens are proof of the interaction and design direction. Their sample text is not a completed education library, a final content specification, or evidence that the experience has been validated with users. The full content set and the details of each visitor journey remain work to do.

### The design principle

The experience should help visitors recognize where to start and move toward useful information without making them understand the site’s database or internal organization. Each path can emphasize different material, while shared subjects and resources remain available where they are relevant.

Choosing an experience is a way to tailor presentation and navigation. It does not, by itself, verify a visitor’s identity or grant access to restricted information.
