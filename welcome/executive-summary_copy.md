# Executive Summary

### Overview

All Things PG (ATPG), as a new nonprofit organization in July 2025, established an early online presence in September 2025 by introducting releasing relelase **<a href="/welcome/glossary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Phase 1</a>** of [www.allthingspg.org](https://www.allthingspg.org).  That fulfilled an urgent need to establish a PG Foundational presense and to begin our work of Raising PG Awarenewss.  The website created visibilty as a nonprofit organization, gave us credility within the a rare-disease community, and provided need informational support to those affected by Pyoderm Gangrenosum (PG). Phase 1 was an early success!

ATPG just celebrated our first year anniversary on July 7, 2026.  It was a time to reflect on some amazing accomplishments and our has expanded well beyond our initial first efforts.  Board Members have engaged in research opportunites with medical professionals clinical trials with pharmaceuticals, drug development participation with the FDA, attending conferences with our own All Things PG booth and printed materials, created connections and industry engagements with other associations, continued our education and advocacy with our website and PFAC activities, as well as numerous newsletters and fundraisers on our website.  Yes, we've had a very busy year, and every board member should be very proud of their efforts and achievemnts as a productive team effort!  The Phase 1 website may be static - but we are not!

We are now sitting at crosroads asking ourselves what to do about our website in light of all these achievents?  Does Phase 1 accurately reflect who we are, and what we've done as an organization today?

**Phase 1** of our webiste was created as a static website at the very begimming of our Journey, and much has changed since. It represented who and what were were at the time, before all this amazing activity, and before we received the **2026 Leo Grant** that is asking for some pretty major changes. This introduces now the need for **Phase 2** enhancements. That is what this documentation site is all about, making a case for Phase 2.

The current website remains a valuable PG Foundation site.  It offers basic information about PG, but was built using a custom cms tool by Krobe, which does not allow for expansion such as adding new features and capabilities.  We can add additional screen, and new content for anything that is already there, like more newsletters or providers, because the tool lets you do that.  But you cannot add new capability if the cms tool does not already offer it as as option.

Our list of accomplishments affected many differrent areas, like building relationships with other organizations, working with pharmaceuticals on drug development, engaging in resaerch, working with medical professionals.  That creates new leads and new opportunities, and new ideas for the website. As our work expands, the website needs to expand with it.  

Information about PG has become more abundant, more clinical. And we need to enhance that information, particularly in the area of early diagnosis, new metrics and new treatments.  We need to collect more data, more statics, and perform more analysis. We need to understand who are users are, and to learn from that.  We need to reach and educate more people. 

One prime example is our Clinic Finder.  That can be a very powerful tool and an excellent resource for anyone who has PG.  Realize too that a person with PG also has a team behind them, perhaps a caregiver a medical professional who may be seeing PG for the first time, a wound care clinic, a Dermatology office, a Rheumatology office, a physical therapist, and mental health councilor.  And more.

So which you think of a search utility to find someone to help, it could be any one of those things. Right now the Clinic Finder is only for doctors, and so far, only 6 accross the entire US. The concept of the tool is great, but as it stands, has very limited usefulness.

A clinic finder should have the abiliity to quickly populate resources, such as wound care centers, medical universities, various types of providers in Dermatoloty, Rheumatology, Mental Health, Physical Therapy and more. It needs to provde search and filter capabilities so users and quickly find the type of results they are looking for. That is 100% impossible given the tool that exists, as well as the custom cms tool that was used to build the website.

Another main goal of Phase 2 is the Knowledge Base (KB), as the central hub for storing content of all types, and the having the ability to search the content for solutions.  Much like the Clinic Finder, the power is in the search and filtering options, and in all the database that is contained.  With a KB, the goal is for a user to enter a natural language expression, and get back a list of articles contained in the KB. The user would then select the article, vs browsing through all the menu items hoping to find what they need. The power is in the search.

For example:  A caregiver recently discovered that her husband has PG.  He is in a lot of pain, and neither one of them knows much about PG wounds.  Even if they have a nurse come once a week, that is not enough, and doesn't do anything to help with the pain, and their next appointment with their doctors is in 3 months.  This is a very realistic scenario, leave both husband and wife unsure what to do.

So they visit www.allthingspg., hoping it truely is all things that can help them. So the caregiver opens up a search and enters:

- How can I help my husband deal with the pain?
- How do I do wound care for my husband?
- Why does PG hurt so much?

The website now switches from an information resource to one that can solve problems by quickly finding answers with expert, vetted, trust information contained within the KB. This is a vastly different approach than browsing information on a static website with no search tool.

This is just one example of why Phase 2 needs to be a major project vs incremental change.  There is one very important thing missing in Phase 1 that is a must have for Phase 2, and that is a **Database**.  Once a website has a databse it is no longer a **static website** consiting of a limited set of prepared HTML pages (vis the cms tool), which has no search capabilities at all.

The website must serve more than just Patients and Caregivers.  Clinicians may need enhanced diagnostic and treatment options, and to read patient stories.  Pharmaceutical may need information about studies and clinical trials, and how to create studies for PG patients.

The goal of All Things PG is to be **all** things related to the rare disease, not just clinical information that you can easily search with Google or an AI tool.  We want to build a growing KB over time and improve search capabilities.

### So why Phase 2?

No doubt, we have grown as an organization, with new goals and new opportunities that have increased beyond what the original website was designed to support. That is hardly a problem for the organzation, only the website, and it is a good problem to have.  

Board Members have collectively participated in research initiatives, discussions on clinical trials, FDA patient centered drug development participation, attending multiple conferences (in-person and virtual),  continued our advocacy efforts, industry engagements, fundraising drives, frequent newsletters and most recently, the establishment of valuable PFACs meetings in multiple locations (in-person and virtual). We have also received a grant to develop new features as a requirement moving forward.

Until now, we have managed to update the website with minor changes, but we are faced with more than just content updates, we need the ability to add new capabilities. That we cannot do in Phase 1.  We have a rather long Wish-List that has been compiled as a group, plus additional Leo requirements for Phase 2.

While the current website continues to remain a valuable asset, it no longer reflects the expanding needs of the organization or the PG community.  Central to this is adding new and expanding functionality, which is not possible within the current cms framework, and only possible with a new architure and by adding a Database to support the new features.  As with any modern website, a database is essential for future enhancements (see discussion on technology for Phase 2).

Our mission remains the same, but our long-term goals for the organization and the website has changed.  It's clear that as ATPG grows, so must the website. Its also a two way street, as in if you build it, we will grow.  How so?  Creating Phase 2 and all the new features it creates interest, new connections and new associations.  It helps more patients, more caregivers and more medical providers and now adding Pharamceuticals. This requires more detailed information, clinical trials, as searchable Clinic Finder, locating more than just providers, such as wound care clinics, PFAC organizations and mental health facilities to name a few. The website is meant to be **All** Things PG, which is not true today, but we will get closer to that objective in Phase 2.  

The website quickly defined who we are as an organization, what we do, and why we exist, while offering basic information about Pyoderma. To be considered All Things PG, we must actually **be** All things to all people affected by this disease and who are visiting the website. The could be Patients, Caregivers, Medical Professionals, Pharmaceuticals or Guests.  We as patients know that PG changes every part of your life: physical, emotional, sprititual, financial, our relationships our our caregivers, wound care etc. Those topics need discussion, and examples.  We have various types of doctors: pain management, mental health, Dermatology, Rheumatology, interactions with drug companies and insurance companies, government agencies, disability, and so forth.  The impact to your life can be devestating, in so many areas.  The website is not limited to basic articles about PG.  

PG affects each of us differently, and in different ways. Some may have only a few issues, others seem to have them all.  It affects our local providers who may have never seen PG before, or Pain Management who may not fully understand how bad it can get.  Some of our patients have a hard time getting pain medication,  pills or patches based on their needs. Without good pain management, many cannot heal while in pain, as pain is stres and contributes to the disease. Because of pain management restrictions, mmany have no quality of life (QOL), and sadly, some have not been able to survive with the level of pain that comes with PG (which is why searching for mental health facilities is so important).  PG often creates severe mobility issues, and we need to know about Physical Therapy and Rehab centers.  Others have become permanentently disabiled and need information how to apply for government benefits.  So clearly, our website should not be restricted to PG disease content, not if we want to be All Things PG.

Phase 2 of our website will be extremly valuable to all of the PG community.  When we consider WHO is asking the questions, we could provide information for that type of user, based on their specific need.  Now we are solving problems, not just presenting information.

Our website may need an update, but our mission remains the same:

The proposed direction for Phase 2 is not simply for the website to keep pace with the organization.  The main objective is to make growth possible by adding a database.  Second to that, once we have a database we can create things like a **fully searchable** Knowledge Base (KB) that continues to grow in content over time.  Our KB could start empty if we wanted to (but why would we want to), but we will start out with all the content from Phase 1 preloaded into Phase 2, and then add additional content to support the expanding wish list items.  Not all content needs to be there day one if the system is designed to add new content in between releases.  We will NEVER have enough content, or a fully populated database, once we realize that we can always add more.  

That raises an important question about features.  If there's a Phase 2 and a Wish-List, what happens if there's not enough time or budget to add all of the items to Phase 2?  That means there will one day be a Phase 3 and a new Wish-List.  Adding content to the KB over time is only possible with a Databaseand a new design called DCMS which stands for Dynamic Content and Menuing System.  A database makes DCM possible, and DCMS makes the KB possible.  

The Phase 2 KB combined with DCMS create the ability to **Curate New Content** at any time, which is the process to search and discover new or updated content via a process (collect, organize, review, and publish).  Perhaps real discoveries were made in diagnosis, treatment, wound care, clinical trials research studies, pain management, new publications, new providers, future conferences, added patient resources, new advocacy initiatives, additional rehab centers and new emerging therapies and drugs. Change is the only constant thing, and so will be updates to curated content, made by a Curator. Currated content goes into a Staging database for review and adjustment, and once vetted, published.  I did not say 'update the website'.  We curate content outside of the scope of the website.  With DCMS, the newly curated content shows up  on the website without any programing at all.  That's part of the function of DCMS.

This all sounds like a pipe dream - except I actually created a prototype database and Proof of Concept (POC) website, proving the design of DCMS works!  That prototype was demo'd to Strom from Leo at the ODS conference in Portland.  Its not a real website only a POC, but will serve as the proven basis to create something real.

To state it very plainly - the KB is the most important feature of Phase 2. Search results will depend on the type of user doing the search as they seek ***SOLUTIONS***, not just information.  What problem are they are trying to solve?  If we can offer detailed answers to our target audience and their specific concern, we are trying to solve their problem, whatever it is, and not just expect them to browse through endless content to find something that helps them.

As design technique in Software Engineering is called Use Cases.  Before you do anything, you decide on who the actors will be, and what are their use cases.  In our world an actor might be a Patient, or a Caregiver etc.  Another word for Use Cases is scenarios, so lets consider these:

- I'm a patient (the actor) and I want to (the use case):
-   discuss a new treatment plan with my doctor
-   learn how to cope with the pain
-   apply for disability
-   cope with the emotion journey of living with PG
-   find mental health providers, or wound care centers near me
 
  I'm a provider and I want to:
-   see research publications from one of my piers
-   treating my first PG patient
-   find a drug that works
-   learn how to diagnose PG
-   learn what mistakes to avoid

- I'm a caregiver and I want to:
-   watch a video on wound care
-   help my husband deal with the pain
-   how to avoid PTSD
-   how do I maintain a PICC line?
-   answer when will this ever end
-   how to maintain a relationship with myspouse

- I'm a drug representative and I want to:
-   how to design a trial for PG patients
-   see who mmight be interested in a clinical trial
-   discover if there are any FDA approved drugs for PG

The number of potential use cases is probably endles, but you get the idea.  Its an outstanding technique to discover why we're building a website, to solve problems, not just present informatoin.  One of our first goals for the KB is the ability to enter natural language expressions and have the database do a full-text index search on content.  Something like 'How do I help my husband copy with the pain', is natural language.  Full text indexing has been around forever, but now with AI you can parse into usuable keywords to produce better results.  Then its a matter of what content exists in the database. You need content to produce search results. The more we load, the better the answers will be.  

### Solving Problems

Another primary goal of Phase 2 is to help people solve problems vs just displaying information. Any google search or AI search can find information. AI is getting very good at that.  We know that every type of visitor arrives to our website with a specific question in mind. The KB is our best answer for providing solutions, not just data.   A searchable knowledge base allows ATPG to organize information around the needs of who is conducting the search, and guide visitors to answers and solutions, not asking them to hunt for information on their own by understanding the structure of our website and menuing system. Another key feature of Phase 2, which is also part of DCMS, is the concept of a Portal.  If we know you're a patient, you're provided a Patient portal, Cargiver etc.  Each portal has a unique menu for that user type. That's hard to imagine at first, but is extremely useful and provides a guided experience.  Additionally, even if the menu items are the same, say on Diagnosis, the actual content can be different.  Content we present to a patient will not be as complex as the diagnostic content we present to a doctor.  Imagine the value of that!

Beyond its value as an educational resource, ATPG has an opportunity to become the central hub of knowledge-sharing within the PG ecosystem. By organizing and curating content about PG, ATPG will solve the information gaps that currently exist with rare diseases.  With rare disease information is often fragmented and difficult to discover trusted resources.  With a curated knowledge platform we can create and present trusted information, vetted by a Board of Directors with  many years of PG experience, and Dr. Ortega as our Medical Director backing us up. 


### In Conclusion Brief Summary

ATPG's opportunity is to evolve from a Phase 1 website containing static information about PG, into a living, beathing, trusted, searchable Knowledge Base of curated and vetted information that is customized for patients, caregivers, providers, pharmacy reps or guests.  Our mission remains to improve the lives of those affected by PG and to raise PG awareness, but we have new goals and long term vision for the Phase 2 website.  We truly want All Things PG to be ALL things that we can do to help those suffering from this challenging rare disease. Phase 2 is a huge leap forward in technology by adding a database, which makes additional futures possible.
