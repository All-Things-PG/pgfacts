# Proof of Concept

### Overview

The Phase 2 Proof of Concept (POC) is a fully functional website as a working prototype, that demonstrates the feasibility, architecture, and core capabilities of the proposed DCMS platform. The whole point of the POC is to go beyond documentation, and prove that the underlying design works in practice.  It also offers a visual opportunity for stakeholders to see something 'real', to get a better understand of what this is all about.  The POC also tests mechanical behaviors such as database access and routing, which validates the architecutre of the website itself, not just the features.

The purpose of THIS POC is simple: demonstrate that a modern, database-driven platform can successfully organize, manage, and present Pyoderma Gangrenosum information in ways that are meaningful to patients, caregivers, healthcare providers, industry partners and guests.

### Why the Proof of Concept Was Created

During the early planning stages of the project, it became apparent that traditional static website approaches would not adequately support the long-term vision for All Things PG. The organization requires a platform capable of managing a growing body of educational content, research information, provider resources, patient experiences, support materials, and future community services. 

The Proof of Concept was created to answer several critical questions:

- Can we build a knowledge base to store all kinds of content for Pyoderma Gangrenosum
- Can content be managed dynamically through a database rather than static web pages?
- Can content be managed dynamically through a database rather than static web pages?
- Can navigation menus be generated automatically from database content?
- Can information be organized around user needs rather than website structure?
- Can the platform scale as content volume and organizational needs grow?
- Can a single system support multiple audiences with different goals and interests?

The POC successfully demonstrates that the answer to each of these questions is yes.

### What the Proof of Concept Demonstrates

The current prototype includes a functional database, dynamic menu generation, content management capabilities, navigation structures, and an operational web interface.

Content is stored within the database and presented dynamically to users based on the Visitor Type. Menus and navigation structures are generated from data rather than being manually hard-coded into individual web pages. This approach enables far greater flexibility, maintainability, and scalability compared to traditional website designs. It also offers the ability to present different information to different types of users.

The POC also validates one of the most important design principles of the DCMS initiative:

**Users should be able to find information based on their specific needs**

Most visitors to a website are there to learn something or solve a problem.  A husband may have recently been diagnosed with PG, and his wife is now his primary caregiver.  Both want to learn about PG, how it was diagnosed and how to treat it.  However, the patient may want to learn about managing the pain.  The caregiver may want to learn about providing emotional support or doing wound care.  A provider may have never treated PG before and may want to read some patient stories, or are interested in clinical resources. The POC proves that content can be different, even for the same menu item.

### Development Effort

The Proof of Concept represents a considerable effort to flush out the design, documentation, development, testing, refinement, and evaluation, and now a presentation of a Documentation Site (pgfacts.org). What began as an initial concept evolved into a functioning system through continuous improvement of the database, content structure, navigation model, and user experience.  In many cases, ideas had to be abandoned as too complex and not feasible. Developing the proof was part of the design process, and the end result is the best of the experiences learned.

The prototype demonstrates not only technical feasibility but also a substantial investment in architectural thinking and practical implementation. Many of the concepts envisioned during the earliest planning stages have now been validated through an operational system.  The 'real' system will not be the POC, but will be built from the concepts that have been proven by the POC.  It is never good to use the POC as the real system.  Quite often short-cuts were made to get something working in test mode.  Its better to start over and build a system based on the final documented design, not the POC. The result is a platform that provides confidence that the broader DCMS vision can be achieved, along with a fully functional (but limited) knowledge base.

### Primary Demonstration Platform

The Proof of Concept serves as the primary demonstration platform for the DCMS initiative, and is an essential part of any proposal.

As organizational leadership, sponsors, partners, researchers, grant reviewers, philanthropic supporters, and potential industry participants evaluate the project, the POC provides tangible evidence that the vision has moved beyond planning and into execution. Rather than asking stakeholders to support an untested idea, the project can be presented as a functionally valid working system that demonstrates real capabilities, real functionality, and real progress.   Many development projects fail.  It significantly reduces project risk because it shows that the foundational technologies, database structures, and user experience concepts have already been validated.

### Future Role

As the project continues to evolve, the Proof of Concept will remain an important demonstration and validation tool. New features, content models, navigation approaches, search functionality, and future services can be tested within the POC before broader implementation. The prototype provides a clear bridge between vision and execution, demonstrating not only what the DCMS project intends to become, but what it has already achieved.

The long-term goal is to transform the POC into a comprehensive information and engagement platform capable of serving the diverse needs of the entire Pyoderma Gangrenosum community while supporting research, education, advocacy, awareness, and future organizational growth.  **The key capability is a searchable knowledge base, which is a powerful tool to display vetted information and resources.**
